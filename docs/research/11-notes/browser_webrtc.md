# Browser WebRTC pitfalls on the Cloudflare Realtime SFU path (as of 2026-10-09)

All sources below were read on 2026-10-09. Items whose primary evidence is older than 2024 are marked "(pre-2024, possibly outdated)". "Unverified" marks claims I could not confirm against a primary source. Each finding is written as symptom / cause / fix where the sources support it.

## Key question 1: Networks — when TURN is required, Cloudflare TURN configuration, and SFU session/track lifetime after a PeerConnection drop

### Takeaway
The Cloudflare SFU is itself a public anycast ICE endpoint, so plain STUN works whenever the client can send UDP to Cloudflare; TURN is the fallback for carriers/corporate networks that drop or block UDP, and Cloudflare's TURN is free when used with the SFU. After a connectivity loss, Cloudflare's own guidance is to **replace** the session (new session, rebuild publications and subscriptions) rather than ICE-restart; the reuse window is 30 s, a track with no packets for 30 s is garbage-collected, and a dead session answers `410 session_error`.

### Cited Findings

**Cloudflare TURN endpoints and credentials**
- Cloudflare TURN hostnames/ports: STUN `stun.cloudflare.com:3478/udp`; TURN/UDP `turn.cloudflare.com:3478` (alternate `443/udp`); TURN/TCP `3478` (alternate `80/tcp`); TURN/TLS `5349/tcp` (alternate `443/tcp`). TLS 1.1–1.3 supported. — [Cloudflare TURN overview](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/turn/)
- Credential API: `POST https://rtc.live.cloudflare.com/v1/turn/keys/$TURN_KEY_ID/credentials/generate-ice-servers` with `Authorization: Bearer $TURN_KEY_API_TOKEN` and body `{"ttl": 86400}`; returns `201` with an `iceServers` object meant to be passed to the front end. The example list contains `stun:stun.cloudflare.com:3478`, `turn:turn.cloudflare.com:3478?transport=udp`, `turn:turn.cloudflare.com:443?transport=udp`, `turn:turn.cloudflare.com:3478?transport=tcp`, `turn:turn.cloudflare.com:80?transport=tcp`, `turns:turn.cloudflare.com:5349?transport=tcp`, `turns:turn.cloudflare.com:443?transport=tcp`. — [Generate credentials](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/turn/generate-credentials/)
- Port-53 warning (symptom: ICE gathering hangs or waits for a timeout): "The alternate port 53 is known to be blocked by web browsers, and the TURN URL will time out if used in browsers." Trickle ICE avoids waiting; otherwise "you might want to filter out the URL with port 53 to avoid waiting for a timeout." The doc's note says the returned list includes the port-53 URL while its example list does not, so filter defensively. — [Generate credentials](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/turn/generate-credentials/)
- TTL guidance: "The `ttl` value can be adjusted to expire the short lived key in a certain amount of time" and "should be larger than the time you'd expect the users to use the TURN service." Credentials "can be refreshed mid-session with `RTCPeerConnection` `setConfiguration()`" and can be revoked early via `POST .../credentials/$USERNAME/revoke` (204). The TURN key is "a long-term secret" that must stay on the server. — [Generate credentials](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/turn/generate-credentials/)
- Maximum credential expiry is 48 hours; the credential JSON does not carry a TTL field. — [TURN FAQ](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/turn/faq/)
- TURN issues IPv4 relay addresses only; client-to-TURN works over IPv4 and IPv6. — [TURN FAQ](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/turn/faq/)
- TURN is "available free of charge when used together with the Realtime SFU"; otherwise $0.05/GB outbound from Cloudflare to the TURN client. — [TURN overview](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/turn/); SFU and TURN share one 1,000 GB/month free tier and "Traffic between TURN and SFU ... is not charged twice." — [SFU pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/platform/pricing/)
- Per-allocation limits (symptom: packet drops): more than 5 new unique IPs/s, more than 5–10 kpps, or more than 50–100 Mbps per TURN allocation — "Hitting these limits will result in packet drops." — [TURN overview](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/turn/)
- Cloudflare's TURN FAQ frames TURN as "when you have a point-to-point connection between two peers and you need to traverse NATs or firewalls"; it does not discuss CGNAT or mobile networks and does not mention `iceTransportPolicy`. — [TURN FAQ](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/turn/faq/)
- The connection-patterns guide only says: STUN is `stun.cloudflare.com:3478`, "TURN can relay traffic when an endpoint's network requires it", and "gather network candidates before sending `pc.localDescription` to your backend" (bounded gathering shown in the cloud-gaming helpers). No `iceTransportPolicy` or `bundlePolicy` guidance. — [Connection patterns](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/get-started/connection-patterns/)

**How the SFU terminates ICE (why STUN usually suffices)**
- Cloudflare Calls/Realtime SFU uses one anycast IP per protocol ("a single IP address for DTLS, ICE, STUN, SRTP, SCTP"); an L4 balancer hashes the 4-tuple, so "ICE checks for IPv4 and IPv6 can land on different servers"; for Chrome the SFU sends DTLS in place of the USE-CANDIDATE STUN check; connections typically establish in 100–250 ms. TURN is described as a separate service. (Post dated 2024-04-04.) — [Cloudflare blog: anycast WebRTC](https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-calls-anycast-webrtc/)

**When TURN is needed (industry estimates, not Cloudflare data)**
- Vendor estimate: roughly one in five WebRTC calls needs TURN (~20% of traffic); the cited studies are not named — unverified figure. — [Metered: what is a TURN server](https://www.metered.ca/blog/what-is-a-turn-server/)
- Mobile carriers place many subscribers behind CGNAT, some NATs behave symmetrically (different external port per destination), and "some carriers ... drop unsolicited UDP packets after short idle windows"; TURN over TCP/TLS on 443 is the recommended fallback. Vendor blog, no measurements. — [ExpressTURN: WebRTC calls fail on mobile networks](https://www.expressturn.com/blog/webrtc-calls-fail-on-mobile-networks)
- `iceTransportPolicy: "relay"` forces relay-only candidates (stable WebRTC API knowledge; MDN reference, not re-fetched): — [MDN RTCPeerConnection() iceTransportPolicy](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/RTCPeerConnection/RTCPeerConnection#icetransportpolicy)

**SFU behaviour when a PeerConnection drops**
- "Track garbage collection: a media track that receives no incoming media packets for 30 seconds is garbage-collected. You must restore the publication and rebuild its subscriptions." — [SFU limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/platform/limits/)
- "Session/track reuse window: after a session loses WebRTC connectivity, reuse has a 30-second window. Replace failed or closed connections immediately, or reconnect with a new session after the window." — [SFU limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/platform/limits/)
- "PeerConnection operations wait up to five seconds for connectivity before timing out." — [SFU limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/platform/limits/)
- `session_error` with HTTP `425`: "finish setup and wait for connection before later operations." With `410`: "check the session ID and endpoint state", "Replace an expired session or closed connection and rebuild any publications and subscriptions. Retrying the old session does not reconnect it." — [SFU error codes](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/observability/error-codes/)
- An unconnected session "can expire before its first track or DataChannel operation" and "Activity on another session does not keep it alive"; later operations then return `410 session_error`. Setup is complete only when the endpoint's connection state is `connected`. — [Sessions and tracks](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/concepts/sessions-tracks/)
- Reconnect guidance: retire the connection rather than restarting ICE, give the replacement "a new application attempt ID", ignore stale responses, keep returned resource IDs for cleanup, and rebuild publications/subscriptions with the new session identifiers. ICE restart is not mentioned anywhere on the page. — [Negotiation and session lifecycle](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/concepts/negotiation/)
- Lost HTTP response: "A lost response leaves the request's outcome unknown. Pause later mutations on that session while recovering." After a lost `/renegotiate` response "If acceptance remains unknown, replace the connection." `correlationId` "does not recover or deduplicate a session." "Application-level duplicate detection does not make an SFU request safe to repeat." — [Negotiation and session lifecycle](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/concepts/negotiation/)
- "Discarding an obsolete connection from application state does not stop SFU forwarding." Use explicit leave/stop operations and application-level expiry, "since browser tabs may close without cleanup." Give each connection attempt an ID so stale responses cannot modify replacement sessions. — [SFU best practices](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/best-practices/)
- `not_found_track_error` means "The requested media publication or DataChannel cannot be found" — verify the publisher is connected and sending packets, then retry; `empty_track_error` means "A media subscription did not receive source media in time." — [SFU error codes](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/observability/error-codes/)

### Inferences
- The phone-on-mobile-data failure is consistent with a carrier that drops UDP or uses symmetric CGNAT: the SFU's anycast IP is public, so NAT alone should not block STUN-only connectivity; a UDP-hostile carrier does. Adding the full Cloudflare TURN list (UDP 3478/443, TCP 3478/80, TLS 5349/443) and letting ICE pick is the fix; keep UDP URLs first so the relay prefers UDP, keep `iceTransportPolicy: "all"` in production, and offer a "relay only" debug toggle to reproduce.
- Because Cloudflare says `410` must be answered by a new session and never mentions ICE restart, the client reconnect path should be: on `connectionState` `failed` (or `disconnected` persisting beyond a few seconds), close the PC, create a new session, republish, resubscribe. Because tracks are garbage-collected 30 s after packets stop, a reconnect that takes longer than 30 s must re-run `tracks/new` for every remote subscription too, and peers must be told the publisher's new session ID.
- A publisher whose camera is "muted" at the transport level (for example iOS backgrounding, see Q2) stops sending RTP; after 30 s its SFU publication disappears and subscribers' tracks go silent without an SFU error on their side. Design the signaling so the publisher republishes and announces a new track name/session after any gap > 30 s.
- Filter port-53 URLs from the credential response before handing it to `RTCPeerConnection`, and generate TURN credentials with a TTL covering the whole meeting (a few hours) or refresh them with `setConfiguration()`.

### Gaps
- Cloudflare publishes no numbers on what share of its SFU clients needs TURN, and no guidance on `iceTransportPolicy`, candidate ordering, or whether the SFU performs ICE restarts; everything about reconnect is "replace the session".
- I found no primary measurement of how often mobile carriers block UDP; the ~20% figure is a vendor estimate.
- Whether a TURN-relayed path to the SFU adds measurable latency vs. direct anycast is not documented.

## Key question 2: iOS Safari (iPhone/iPad) pitfalls

### Takeaway
iOS Safari suspends capture on backgrounding, lock, Siri and phone calls; the microphone track can stay `muted` forever with no `ended` event, so the app must detect it and re-run `getUserMedia` after a user tap. Remote audio needs `playsinline` plus an explicit audio-unlock gesture (ringer switch and AudioContext suspension add more traps). Speaker selection arrived only in Safari 26 on iOS, `getDisplayMedia` is still absent on iOS through 26.5, and iOS 26.0 shipped a WebSocket regression (HTTP 400 when the server offers HTTP/3 or extended CONNECT) that was fixed in 26.1.

### Cited Findings

**Backgrounding, lock screen, phone calls**
- Video lost when the user backgrounds the browser or locks/unlocks during a call (iOS 17 betas; rejoining gave no video until both sides re-enabled cameras); resolved fixed, reporter says fixed in iOS 17.1. — [WebKit bug 259337](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=259337)
- "Mic input is not returned to the mediaStream after a GSM call": the audio track fires `mute` when a call takes the mic, never fires `unmute` or `ended` afterwards, camera recovers; status NEW/Blocker, last modified 2023-01-23. Workaround: show a "microphone problem" prompt, and on click restart the track and `replaceTrack` on all peer connections, because "visibility and similar browser events do not fire after a dismissed call." — [WebKit bug 213853](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=213853)
- Same family: mic silent after a call, `getUserMedia` returns a normal-looking but dataless stream, only closing the tab fixed it (iOS 13.3.1, open). — [WebKit bug 208209](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=208209); a WebKit engineer: "if a phone call happens, the page microphone capture will be suspended, track will be muted and the web page is now suspendible"; re-calling `getUserMedia` after the track ended fixes it. — [WebKit bug 204681](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=204681); "audio capture will fail as the PSTN call has higher priority. Audio capture can succeed through getUserMedia once the PSTN call is finished." — [WebKit bug 240651](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=240651)
- Tab switching can leave video capture muted; community workaround: on return to the page check whether tracks are `muted`/`ended`, re-`getUserMedia`, then `RTCRtpSender.replaceTrack`. — [WebKit bug 208516](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=208516)
- WebKit ties camera capture to document visibility (long-running ticket on muting the video source when the document is hidden). (pre-2024, possibly outdated) — [WebKit bug 172200](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=172200)
- Setting a *remote* audio track's `enabled = false` ended the track on iOS 13.1; workaround `replaceTrack(null)` on the sender side. (pre-2024, possibly outdated) — [WebKit bug 202405](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=202405)

**Autoplay, audio unlock, AudioContext**
- Safari autoplays MediaStream media only if the page is already capturing or playing audio; other audio requires a user gesture; `playsinline` is required for inline video; iOS limits simultaneous audio-bearing elements (reader comment); `beforeunload` is not fired, use `pagehide`; Safari randomises device IDs per page load (stable within a session since iOS 12.2). (Article 2018, updated 2019; pre-2024, possibly outdated.) — [webrtcHacks: Guide to WebRTC with Safari in the Wild](https://webrtchacks.com/guide-to-safari-webrtc/)
- Microphone permission does not unlock the speaker: a WebKit comment states the user "gave permission to capture from the microphone, not to use the speaker." (2017; pre-2024, possibly outdated) — [WebKit bug 177292](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=177292)
- AudioContext is suspended when the screen locks or the page is minimised; `resume()` after a screen lock "sometimes works and sometimes it does not" (iOS 15.x). — [WebKit bug 239996](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=239996)
- LiveKit user report: after a token refresh/remount Safari flags the new incoming audio track and mutes it until the user "physically touches the screen." — [LiveKit community thread](https://community.livekit.io/t/seeking-react-patterns-to-gracefully-handle-safaris-strict-autoplay-policies-and-background-audio-suspension-for-ios-users/1649)
- Third-party guide: iOS Safari blocks Web Audio playback when the ringer switch is on silent/vibrate. Unverified against WebKit sources. — [abratabia: mobile audio issues](https://www.abratabia.com/web-game-audio/mobile-audio-issues.php)
- General background on autoplay policies silencing remote WebRTC audio (2018; pre-2024, possibly outdated). — [webrtcHacks: autoplay restrictions and WebRTC](https://webrtchacks.com/autoplay-restrictions-and-webrtc/)

**Feature availability (Safari 18.4 → 26)**
- Safari 18.4 (2025-03-31): Speaker Selection / `setSinkId` **macOS only**; MediaSession capture-mute API (unmuting needs user activation); Screen Wake Lock now works in Home Screen Web Apps on iOS/iPadOS 18.4. — [WebKit Features in Safari 18.4](https://webkit.org/blog/16574/webkit-features-in-safari-18-4/)
- Daily.co: Safari < 18.4 has no `setSinkId` or audio-output enumeration; 18.4 introduced it but "does not work reliably". — [Daily docs: setOutputDeviceAsync](https://docs.daily.co/reference/daily-js/instance-methods/set-output-device-async)
- Safari 26.0 (2025-09-15): "Speaker Selection API on iOS and iPadOS"; `generateKeyFrame` accepts a `rid`; **removed the `fec` and `rtx` encoding parameters**; `configurationchange` fires when a microphone's echo-cancellation mode changes; `enumerateDevices` no longer checks device permission; switching from speaker to receiver now works on the first attempt; "WebSocket over HTTP/2 and HTTP/3" added; every site added to the Home Screen now opens as a web app by default (manifest no longer required); media elements on iPadOS now honour developer volume changes; PiP exits when the video element is removed. — [WebKit Features in Safari 26.0](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/)
- `getDisplayMedia` on Safari iOS is marked not supported through 26.5 (desktop Safari supported since 13). — [caniuse getDisplayMedia](https://caniuse.com/mdn-api_mediadevices_getdisplaymedia); a 2023 comment on the "fixed" WebKit ticket says it still did not work on iOS/iPadOS 16.5. — [WebKit bug 231455](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=231455)
- Screen Wake Lock: Safari iOS 16.4 onward; previously broken in home-screen (PWA) mode until the 18.4 fix above. — [caniuse wake-lock](https://caniuse.com/wake-lock); — [WebKit bug 254545](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=254545)
- Safari supports H.264 and VP8 simulcast since 12.2; H.264 is the default because it is hardware-accelerated and "increases the battery life by up to an hour compared to VP8" at 720p; H.264 and VP8 can mix on one Unified-Plan connection. (2019; pre-2024, possibly outdated but still the baseline) — [WebKit blog: On the Road to WebRTC 1.0, Including VP8](https://webkit.org/blog/8672/on-the-road-to-webrtc-1-0-including-vp8/)
- Community report: Safari H.264 simulcast works but `scaleResolutionDownBy` is ignored for screen-capture sources. Undated forum post, unverified. — [mediasoup forum: browser idiosyncrasies with simulcast](https://mediasoup.discourse.group/t/browser-idiosyncracies-with-simulcast-and-svc/5493)
- Safari did not honour `degradationPreference: "maintain-framerate"` while Chromium and Firefox did (2023). — [WebKit bug 262609](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=262609)

**Home-screen web apps**
- Camera stream does not work after the site is added to the Home Screen when the manifest `display` is `standalone`/`fullscreen` (iPhone 15 Pro, iOS 17.4.1; no error thrown); status NEW. — [WebKit bug 273938](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=273938)
- 2025 report of a PWA being re-prompted for camera permission intermittently although permission was granted. — [Apple Discussions thread](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256081579)
- Safari 26.0 opens home-screen sites as web apps by default, so the standalone-mode bugs above now apply to any user who "adds to Home Screen". — [WebKit Features in Safari 26.0](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/)

**WebSocket signaling on iOS**
- iOS/iPadOS/macOS 26.0 regression: `wss://` handshakes fail with HTTP 400 and never reach the server when Safari attempts WebSocket over HTTP/3; workarounds were disabling HTTP/3 (QUIC) or turning off `SETTINGS_ENABLE_CONNECT_PROTOCOL` on the server; "underlying framework issue got fixed in 26.1" (2025-11-05), confirmed by a reporter. — [WebKit bug 298616](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=298616)
- Background WebSockets: on iOS 15 the socket closed once the user switched apps; a `visibilitychange` reconnect works but after long inactivity `onerror`/`onclose` are not delivered, so the page cannot see the dead connection; one poster says a related issue "Seems to be resolved in Beta 5" of iOS 26 (unconfirmed for the release). — [Apple forum: WebSocket issue in iOS 15 Safari](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/696310); — [Apple forum: Websockets in iOS26](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/792842)
- Apple DTS: the factor is "running/suspended", iOS usually suspends an app shortly after backgrounding; web pages cannot request background modes. — [Apple forum thread 716118](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/716118)
- Safari 26.5 reportedly fixed a WebRTC flaw that could crash a process while certain pages load (secondary coverage; check Apple security notes). — [apfeltalk](https://www.apfeltalk.de/magazin/news/safari-26-5-schliesst-webkit-sicherheitsluecken-und-verhindert-abstuerze/)

### Inferences
- Treat every local track on iOS as revocable: listen to `mute`, `unmute`, `ended`, `visibilitychange` and `pageshow`; when the page becomes visible and a track is still `muted` or `ended`, show a one-tap "Re-enable microphone/camera" control that calls `getUserMedia` again and `replaceTrack` on the publish PC, since WebKit requires user activation and events may not fire after a dismissed call.
- Combine the audio unlock into the Join gesture: on the user's tap, call `play()` on every remote `<audio>`/`<video playsinline>` element and `resume()` any AudioContext (needed for client-side active-speaker detection). Re-check `AudioContext.state` and element `paused` state on every `visibilitychange` to `visible`, and show a "tap to hear audio" overlay when still blocked.
- Because iOS stops sending camera RTP in the background, the SFU will garbage-collect the publication after 30 s (Q1). On foregrounding, republish rather than assume the old track exists.
- Since Cloudflare's edge negotiates HTTP/3 by default, Durable-Object WebSocket signaling could have failed for iOS 26.0 users (the bug is fixed in 26.1, which most devices run by October 2026); keep a REST fallback or a clear "update iOS" message for 26.0.
- Do not expose "share screen" on iOS (feature-detect `navigator.mediaDevices.getDisplayMedia`). Do expose `setSinkId` only when `HTMLMediaElement.prototype.setSinkId` exists (Safari 26+ on iOS); iOS routes speaker/earpiece via the system by default.
- Safari 26 silently ignores `fec`/`rtx` in `sendEncodings`; code that sets them must not treat the absence as an error.

### Gaps
- I found no webkit.org post describing WebRTC changes in Safari 26.1–26.5 beyond security fixes; iOS 26-specific capture/backgrounding behaviour is not documented and the 2020–2023 bugs above have no maintainer confirmation of fixes.
- Picture-in-Picture for WebRTC video on iOS: no source found beyond the 26.0 "PiP exits when element removed" fix.
- Whether `setSinkId` on iOS 26 works reliably in practice: only Daily's "unreliable" note for 18.4 on macOS; nothing for iOS.
- H.264 profile/level constraints on iOS (which `profile-level-id` values Safari offers/accepts) were not covered by any fetched source.

## Key question 3: Firefox pitfalls

### Takeaway
Firefox gained `setCodecPreferences` in 128, H.264 simulcast only in 136 (before that simulcast needed VP8), `degradationPreference` in 138, and AV1 by default in 2025; it still does not implement `MediaStreamTrack.contentHint`. `setParameters()` is asynchronous, `active:false` does not change the SDP, and audio `active` was unimplemented.

### Cited Findings
- `RTCRtpTransceiver.setCodecPreferences` resolved fixed for Firefox 128; the method "limits which decoders the browser considers during negotiation, rather than controlling what gets sent directly." — [Bugzilla 1396922](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1396922); — [Mozilla WebRTC blog: cross-browser codec choice](https://blog.mozilla.org/webrtc/cross-browser-support-for-choosing-webrtc-codecs/)
- `MediaStreamTrack.contentHint`: Chrome 70, Safari 12.1, Firefox "No" on desktop and Android per MDN compat data (mirror). Feature-detect with `"contentHint" in track`. — [MDN compat mirror for contentHint](https://docs.w3cub.com/dom/mediastreamtrack/contenthint)
- H.264 simulcast landed in Firefox 136 (bug opened 10 years earlier: "only a single H264 encoder per webrtc.org channel"). — [Bugzilla 1210175](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1210175)
- Firefox 2025 review (2026-01-13): simulcast added for H.264 and AV1; dependency-descriptor header support (also for H.264); AV1 on by default; H.264 temporal layers; `RTCDegradationPreference` functions implemented (bug 1329847); `RTCRtpEncodingParameters.codec` implemented; remaining mandatory `RTCIceCandidatePairStats` fields implemented; `SendKeyFrameRequest` no longer rejects based on transceiver state; DataChannels in workers; dcsctp migration. No version numbers given. — [Mozilla WebRTC blog: Firefox WebRTC 2025](https://blog.mozilla.org/webrtc/firefox-webrtc-2025/)
- `degradationPreference` landed on the Firefox 138 branch per search-result summary of Bugzilla 1329847 (version unverified by me). — [Bugzilla 1329847](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1329847)
- `setParameters({encodings:[{active:false}]})`: maintainer (Byron Campen) explains `setParameters()` is async so `getParameters()` right after shows the old value; `active` does not affect SDP (the `~rid` pause syntax needs RFC 7728, which browsers do not support; "if Chrome signals pause in SDP, it violates the spec"); `active` for **audio** was not implemented and the bug was repurposed to track that (duplicate of 1813848). — [Bugzilla 1834806](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1834806)
- Firefox 82 sent all simulcast layers at the full `b=TIAS` bitrate while Safari dropped higher layers. (2020; pre-2024, possibly outdated) — [Bugzilla 1676879](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1676879)
- RIDs should be set in `addTransceiver({sendEncodings})`, not later via `setParameters()`; spec discussion noted RIDs "sometimes work and sometimes don't" when changed later. (2016; pre-2024, possibly outdated) — [W3C public-webrtc thread](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-webrtc/2016Feb/0034.html)
- `RTCPeerConnection.connectionState` is Baseline "Widely available ... since May 2023", so Firefox supports it (exact first version not extracted). — [MDN connectionState](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/RTCPeerConnection/connectionState)
- Firefox mochitest for simulcast `setParameters` exists as a reference for expected behaviour. — [searchfox test_peerConnection_setParameters.html](https://arai.searchfox.org/firefox-main/source/dom/media/webrtc/tests/mochitests/test_peerConnection_setParameters.html)

### Inferences
- With "H.264 first, then VP8", a Firefox < 136 publisher would negotiate H.264 but could not simulcast it; ESR/old builds should be served VP8 or `setCodecPreferences` should be checked per-browser. Firefox ≥ 136 handles H.264 simulcast.
- Because `contentHint` is missing in Firefox, use `degradationPreference` (Firefox ≥ 138) on the sender for screen-share "maintain-resolution"; feature-detect both.
- Always `await sender.setParameters(...)` and re-read `getParameters()` inside the resolved promise; never key UI state off a synchronous read.
- Pausing a simulcast layer with `active:false` is invisible to the SFU's SDP; the SFU simply stops receiving that RID, so subscribers on that RID need `asciibetical` fallback (Q5) or a `tracks/update` to another RID.

### Gaps
- No Firefox-specific documentation of `getStats()` differences versus Chrome beyond the 2025 candidate-pair stats completion.
- No source on Firefox `mute`/`unmute` event behaviour for remote tracks beyond the spec discussion in Q4.
- Firefox version numbers for the 2025 items (AV1 default, dependency descriptor) were not stated in the blog post.

## Key question 4: Chrome / Android pitfalls

### Takeaway
On Android Chrome the Media Engagement Index does not apply (desktop only), so remote audio needs a user gesture unless the site is installed to the home screen. Chromium mutes/unmutes remote tracks based on packet arrival (crbug 941740), which conflicts with the spec and makes `mute`/`unmute` unreliable as a "media is flowing" signal; use `getStats()` instead. `degradationPreference` is still shipped (not removed). H.264 hardware availability on Android varies and CPU adaptation can push a stream onto an unsupported profile, silently stopping video.

### Cited Findings

## Key question 5: General — negotiation, SDP, Opus, simulcast layers, limits, DataChannels, scale

### Takeaway
The Cloudflare SFU replaces glare handling with a hard rule: serialize every mutation per session, finish the SDP exchange before the next one, and never treat browser `signalingState: "stable"` as completion. Simulcast fallback is opt-in (`asciibetical`) and the RIDs `f/h/q` already sort best-first. Opus `fmtp` flags are receiver preferences, not encoder commands. Limits are 50 API req/s per session, 64 tracks per request, 30 s GC, $0.05/GB egress after 1,000 GB.

### Cited Findings

**Negotiation and ordering**
- Mutations on one session ("track and DataChannel creation, updates, closure, and renegotiation") must be serialized; different sessions can run concurrently. "Each mutation finishes only after its API request and SDP exchange succeed. Reaching `signalingState: "stable"` in the browser does not finish the backend's work." Use one shared queue per session. — [Negotiation and session lifecycle](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/concepts/negotiation/)
- `requiresImmediateRenegotiation: true` means "complete that exchange before starting another mutation on the session." Overlapping requests: "The SFU can reject the conflicting request with HTTP `406`." — [Negotiation and session lifecycle](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/concepts/negotiation/)
- "A browser's `signalingState` becoming `stable` does not confirm that the backend has submitted the answer." Subscribe flow: `tracks/new` with `location: "remote"` and no local offer → `setRemoteDescription(offer)` → `createAnswer` → gather → `PUT /renegotiate`; "Continue only after the API accepts it." Publish flow: `addTransceiver(track, {direction: "sendonly"})`, create offer, gather, send `mid`s with `tracks/new`; the returned answer needs no `/renegotiate`. — [Connection patterns](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/get-started/connection-patterns/)
- Media-close sequence: `transceiver.stop()` on each, `createOffer`/`setLocalDescription`, then `PUT /tracks/close` with `tracks: [{mid}]`, `sessionDescription`, `force: false`; apply the returned answer "even if some tracks failed"; or `force: true` with no SDP (no `/renegotiate`), which "can leave records in session information." — [Connection patterns](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/get-started/connection-patterns/)
- "A timed-out API request may still finish." Closing known tracks does not cancel it. "Forced closure does not finish the abandoned SDP exchange." — [Negotiation and session lifecycle](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/concepts/negotiation/)
- "A successful HTTP response can contain errors for individual tracks. Check each result before recording a publication or subscription as ready." A track's `mid` belongs to its connection; a subscriber receives the publication on a different `mid`. — [Sessions and tracks](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/concepts/sessions-tracks/)
- `invalid_session_description`: "Check the SDP, transceivers, `mid` values, and codec support. Complete the outstanding exchange, serialize later mutations, and don't replay a finished answer." `payload_too_large`: reduce request size "keeping the complete generated SDP". — [SFU error codes](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/observability/error-codes/)
- Perfect negotiation (polite/impolite peer, `rollback`) is the standard glare pattern for peer-to-peer; stable MDN reference, not re-fetched. — [MDN: Perfect negotiation](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebRTC_API/Perfect_negotiation)

**Simulcast**
- Publisher SDP carries `a=simulcast:send f;h;q` and `a=rid:<name> send`; browser uses `addTransceiver` `sendEncodings` with `rid` and `scaleResolutionDownBy` 1/2/4. "Configure the publishing endpoint before creating its offer." — [SFU simulcast](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/features/simulcast/)
- Subscription `simulcast` object: `preferredRid`, `priorityOrdering` (`none` = keep sending `preferredRid` even without bandwidth; `asciibetical` = "a is most desirable and z is least desirable"), `ridNotAvailable` (`none` | `asciibetical`). "Both default to `none`, so no automatic fallback happens unless you set `asciibetical`." Each subscription has its own configuration. Switch with `PUT .../tracks/update` using the receiving `mid`; `update_track_error` if the track is not configured for simulcast. The "simulcast echo sample" is legacy (puts the SFU token in the browser). — [SFU simulcast](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/features/simulcast/)
- Supported codecs: video H.264, H.265, VP8, VP9, AV1; audio Opus, G.711 A/µ-law. — [SFU limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/platform/limits/)

**Limits and pricing**
- API rate: "50 requests per second. The rate limit is per session rather than per app"; up to 64 tracks per `tracks/new`; tracks per session "No fixed upper bound"; WebSocket audio ingest message ≤ 32 KB; one `canReply` subscriber per DataChannel. — [SFU limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/platform/limits/)
- Egress $0.05/GB for SFU and TURN, first 1,000 GB/month free (shared); ingress free "even if no client subscribes"; page last updated 2026-09-22. — [SFU pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/platform/pricing/)
- Stale page: the old `/calls/limits/` page states 15 s for `waitForAck` whereas current docs say 30 s — the `/calls/` tree is outdated. — [old Calls limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/calls/limits/) vs [SFU limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/platform/limits/)

**DataChannels**
- Create with `POST .../datachannels/new` (`location: "local"` + `dataChannelName`; subscribers use `location: "remote"` + publisher `sessionId`); in the browser `pc.createDataChannel(name, { negotiated: true, id: channelId })` with the SFU-returned id (publisher and subscriber ids may differ). "Negotiated DataChannels do not communicate these settings to the browser automatically." `ordered`/`maxRetransmits`/`maxPacketLifeTime` must be mirrored by subscribers; "Set at most one of `maxRetransmits` and `maxPacketLifeTime`." `waitForAck`: first subscriber message within 30 s or the gated channel is torn down. `canReply`: one subscriber per publisher channel; replies go only to the publisher. Close: `PUT .../datachannels/close`; "Closure needs no SDP exchange." — [SFU DataChannels](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/features/datachannels/)

**Opus and SDP munging**
- `cbr`, `useinbandfec`, `usedtx` in SDP "only express that the decoder on the receiver prefers cbr, fec or dtx, but in no way control what the encoder on either side must use." — [W3C public-webrtc-logs Jan 2021](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-webrtc-logs/2021Jan/0080.html)
- Opus in-band FEC is RECOMMENDED for WebRTC, provides single-frame redundancy only (does not cover bursts), ~20–30% overhead, and "should only be activated if network conditions warrant it, or upon explicit application request." — [RFC 8854](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8854.html)
- Per-sender bitrate caps (`maxBitrate` in encodings) do not appear in SDP; "expect absolutely nothing at SDP level"; verify in `chrome://webrtc-internals`. — [mediasoup forum: bitrate settings](https://mediasoup.discourse.group/t/help-me-understand-bitrate-settings/369)
- `maxaveragebitrate`: when both sides specify a value "the lowest will prevail" (FreeSWITCH behaviour; browser behaviour may differ). — [FreeSWITCH mod_opus](https://freeswitch.org/confluence/display/FREESWITCH/mod_opus)

**Cloudflare security/authorization best practices**
- "A room name, session ID, track name, or adapter ID is a locator rather than proof of permission"; keep the App Secret in a Worker secret; do not expose an unrestricted SFU proxy; show reconnects to the user, limit retries, offer a manual recovery action. — [SFU best practices](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/best-practices/)

### Inferences
- The project's RIDs `q/h/f` with `scaleResolutionDownBy` 4/2/1 are already in the right order for `asciibetical` (f < h < q, so `f` is "most desirable"); set `priorityOrdering: "asciibetical"` and `ridNotAvailable: "asciibetical"` on every subscription, otherwise a subscriber pinned to `f` while the publisher drops that layer (CPU/bandwidth) gets no video.
- Glare cannot happen between browser and SFU in the perfect-negotiation sense because every negotiation is initiated through a backend request; what replaces it is the per-session queue in the Durable Object: one in-flight mutation per session, with `406` treated as "retry after the queue drains", and `requiresImmediateRenegotiation` handled before dequeuing the next item.
- Avoid SDP munging entirely: Cloudflare validates SDP (`invalid_session_description`) and the `fmtp` flags do not steer the browser's encoder anyway. Control Opus via `getUserMedia` constraints (`echoCancellation`, `noiseSuppression`, `autoGainControl`) and sender `setParameters` (`maxBitrate`); leave DTX/FEC at defaults unless measured loss justifies FEC.
- `replaceTrack` keeps the negotiated codec and `mid`, so swapping camera ↔ screen share on one sender is cheap, but switching between an H.264-only and a VP8-only source would need a new transceiver and a `tracks/new`; keep camera and screen share on separate publications. (Stable WebRTC behaviour; no fetched source.)
- Egress at scale: with N participants each subscribing to N−1 camera streams, egress grows ~N²; at $0.05/GB after 1,000 GB the cost lever is the simulcast layer: subscribe to `q` for thumbnails and `f` only for the pinned/active speaker, and switch via `tracks/update` rather than resubscribing.
- Active-speaker detection: none of the fetched Cloudflare pages mention server-side audio levels or dominant-speaker events, so compute it client-side (per-remote-track `AudioContext` analyser, which on iOS needs the unlock gesture from Q2, or `getStats()` `inbound-rtp.audioLevel`) and share the result over the signaling WebSocket.

### Gaps
- Cloudflare does not document an active-speaker/audio-level feature, bandwidth caps per subscription, or any per-app concurrency limits beyond the 50 req/s per session.
- No source quantifies browser bandwidth-estimation behaviour against the Cloudflare SFU (for example REMB/TWCC settings) or recommended `maxBitrate` per RID.
- Browser handling of Opus `usedtx` (Chrome enables DTX via SDP, Firefox/Safari behaviour) was not confirmed by a primary source.
