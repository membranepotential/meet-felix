# Eight changes before meet-felix records anyone

Beyond the TURN credentials added today, the design misses eight things that must change in the app, and about twenty that need a test or a note. The most concrete gap is in code: `sfu-api` sends every subscription with `priorityOrdering: "none"`, so when a subscriber's bandwidth drops the SFU keeps pushing the pinned `f` layer instead of falling back, because Cloudflare's docs say no fallback happens unless both fallback fields are `asciibetical` ([SFU simulcast](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/features/simulcast/)). The second is structural: `RoomObject` stores one JSON record, scheduled links live for 60 days, and the devlog already notes that rooms stored before today's `Joined` change no longer deserialize; with Cloudflare's eventually consistent rollout, where a newer Worker calls an older Durable Object "for seconds to minutes" ([DO known issues](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/known-issues/)), every shape change without a versioned record kills every scheduled meeting. On the browser side, the session replacement from D33 recreates peer connections, and audio that starts outside a user gesture is blocked on Android and iOS; iOS also leaves a microphone `muted` forever after a phone call with no event, so D14's event-driven recovery needs a one-tap fallback. For phase 3 the `RecorderObject` design must treat the adapter stream as neither gapless nor exactly-once, keep silent chunks away from Whisper (which hallucinates on 62% of 30-second non-speech inputs), build Ogg pages in 48 kHz granule units, and point the adapters at the Worker directly rather than through the Pages hop. Legally, D15's "Join and accept recording" is a good explicit act, but German guidance wants a documented consent record and a full notice that names the owner as controller, Cloudflare as processor, and the Workers AI subprocessors in the US and England; D18's "keep indefinitely" contradicts the GDD's "delete audio once the transcript exists" and leaves the notice without a retention period. Everything else, from the Workers Free plan's hard daily walls to KV's 60-second negative cache colliding with recycled words, is a watch item with a known workaround.

## The media path works until bandwidth drops or a phone rings

The SFU rules the design already follows (serialize mutations per session, replace dead sessions, close tracks explicitly) are right; the pitfalls lie in what happens under stress. **Simulcast fallback is only half enabled.** Cloudflare documents two fields on a subscription: `ridNotAvailable` picks a neighbour when the preferred layer does not exist, and `priorityOrdering` lets the SFU step down when bandwidth is short; with `priorityOrdering: "none"` the SFU keeps "sending `preferredRid` even without bandwidth", and "Both default to `none`, so no automatic fallback happens unless you set `asciibetical`" ([SFU simulcast](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/features/simulcast/)). `crates/sfu-api/src/client.rs` line 329 sets `priority_ordering: LayerFallback::None` and only `rid_not_available` to `Asciibetical`, so the `f`/`h`/`q` naming from D11, chosen precisely so that `f < h < q` sorts best-first, does its job only when a publisher drops a layer, not when a subscriber's link degrades. The fix is one line in `sfu-api` and a note in D11. Confirmed by the primary doc and the code.

**Remote audio can stay silent after a D33 reset.** Chrome allows muted autoplay always; sound needs a user interaction, and on mobile the desktop Media Engagement Index exception does not apply ([Chrome autoplay policy](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay)). WebKit's position is older but unchanged in substance: microphone permission is "permission to capture from the microphone, not to use the speaker" ([WebKit bug 177292](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=177292), 2017), and a LiveKit user reports Safari muting a fresh incoming audio track after a remount until the user "physically touches the screen" ([LiveKit community thread](https://community.livekit.io/t/seeking-react-patterns-to-gracefully-handle-safaris-strict-autoplay-policies-and-background-audio-suspension-for-ios-users/1649), community report). D33 replaces the subscriber peer connection without a user gesture, so any `<audio>` element the web shell creates for the new tracks may never play. The practical rule is to unlock in the Join tap (call `play()` on every remote element and `resume()` the `AudioContext` that D30's level bars need), keep those elements alive across a reset and only swap `srcObject`, and re-check element `paused` and `AudioContext.state` on every `visibilitychange`, because the context is suspended on screen lock and `resume()` "sometimes works and sometimes it does not" ([WebKit bug 239996](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=239996)). The autoplay policy is confirmed; the interaction with D33 is an inference from it.

**iOS microphones die quietly after a phone call.** D14 reacquires devices on `visibilitychange`, `mute` and `ended`. WebKit's own bug says the audio track fires `mute` when a call takes the microphone and never fires `unmute` or `ended`, and that "visibility and similar browser events do not fire after a dismissed call" ([WebKit bug 213853](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=213853), status NEW, last touched 2023); a WebKit engineer confirms capture is suspended and the page becomes suspendible, and that re-calling `getUserMedia` fixes it ([WebKit bug 204681](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=204681); [WebKit bug 240651](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=240651)). Since WebKit requires user activation for capture, the shell needs a visible "Re-enable microphone" control whenever a local track is still `muted` or `ended` after the page becomes visible, with `getUserMedia` and `replaceTrack` behind the tap. These are pre-2024 bugs without a maintainer-confirmed fix; spike 4 on a real iPhone decides whether they still apply, but the control costs little either way.

Three smaller browser items are watch-only. Safari 26.0 shipped a regression where `wss://` handshakes fail with HTTP 400 when the browser attempts WebSocket over HTTP/3; the fix landed in 26.1 ([WebKit bug 298616](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=298616)), and since Cloudflare's edge negotiates HTTP/3, a guest on 26.0 cannot reach `RoomObject` at all; a clear "update iOS" message on a failed upgrade is enough. Safari 26 also removed the `fec` and `rtx` encoding parameters ([WebKit Features in Safari 26.0](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/)); the web shell does not set them, so nothing breaks today. Firefox gained H.264 simulcast only in version 136 ([Bugzilla 1210175](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1210175)), so an older Firefox negotiating H.264 first under D12 publishes without layers; and on Android, Chrome's CPU adaptation can push an H.264 stream onto a profile the hardware encoder lacks, after which "the remote side would simply stop receiving video" ([BlogGeek.me](https://bloggeek.me/webrtc-h264-video-codec-hardware-support/), secondary). Both argue for checking `RTCRtpSender.getCapabilities('video')` per device and falling back to VP8 for simulcast when H.264 is absent or `outbound-rtp` reports `qualityLimitationReason: "cpu"` repeatedly; that is a test item, not a change. The design's choice not to use `track.muted` as a liveness signal is right: Chromium mutes remote tracks on packet arrival in a way the spec editors called a web-compat problem that "has been open for almost 5 years" ([W3C public-webrtc, Dec 2023](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-webrtc/2023Dec/0026.html)); poll `inbound-rtp` via `getStats()` instead. In Firefox, `setParameters()` is asynchronous and `active: false` for audio was never implemented ([Bugzilla 1834806](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1834806)), so the shell must read `getParameters()` only inside the resolved promise.

On the SFU side the facts confirm the existing design and add two cautions. Overlapping mutations on one session can be rejected with HTTP 406 ([Negotiation and session lifecycle](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/concepts/negotiation/)); `sfu-api` classifies a 406 as `Transient` unless the error code is `session_error`, which is the right split, and the `RoomObject`'s per-session queue is the glare mechanism this architecture replaces perfect negotiation with. "A timed-out API request may still finish" and "Forced closure does not finish the abandoned SDP exchange" (same page), so a timeout must pause further mutations on that session rather than retry; D33's replacement covers the unknown-outcome case. TURN credentials expire after at most 48 hours and the JSON carries no TTL field ([TURN FAQ](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/turn/faq/)); the 24-hour TTL from `/api/ice` is fine for any realistic meeting, and `setConfiguration()` can refresh mid-session ([Generate credentials](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/turn/generate-credentials/)). A TURN allocation drops packets above roughly 5–10 kpps or 50–100 Mbps ([TURN overview](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/turn/)), far above one phone's load. Cloudflare publishes no share of clients that need TURN; the ~20% figure is a vendor estimate ([Metered](https://www.metered.ca/blog/what-is-a-turn-server/), unverified).

## A schema change can kill every scheduled link

The platform's sharpest edge is how deploys meet stored state. Deployments "restart Durable Objects and clear in-memory state" ([Access DO storage](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/best-practices/access-durable-objects-storage/)), "Code updates disconnect all WebSockets" ([DO WebSockets best practice](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/best-practices/websockets/)), and rollout is eventually consistent, so a newer Worker can call an older DO "for seconds to minutes" and the docs ask you to keep Worker↔DO APIs compatible in both directions ([DO known issues](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/known-issues/)). The devlog records that rooms stored before today's change "do not deserialize (the `Joined` shape changed)" and that "a deploy during a live meeting breaks that meeting". Live meetings are a nuisance; scheduled rooms are the real loss, because D8 keeps them for 60 days and the owner creates links days ahead. **Every `Record` shape change without serde defaults, a version field, or a migration path silently turns every scheduled link into a dead room.** The change is in `meeting-core`'s stored types: tolerate unknown fields, default new ones, and add a version so the `RoomObject` can migrate or at least report an unreadable room instead of failing the join. The core-first rule (D20) suggests a test that deserializes the previous release's fixtures. This is confirmed by primary docs and the project's own log.

**The Workers Free plan cannot carry the recorder.** On Free, every limit fails hard: "If you exceed any limit, further operations of that type fail with an error" ([DO pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/)), and Workers AI on Free has no overage at all ([Workers AI pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/)). The walls are 10 ms CPU per invocation, 100,000 requests per day shared with Pages Functions ([Pages Functions pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/functions/pricing/)), 13,000 GB-s per day of DO duration, 1,000 KV writes and 1,000 KV lists per day ([Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/)), and 10,000 neurons per day, which at 46.63 neurons per minute for `whisper-large-v3-turbo` is about 214 audio minutes ([Workers AI pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/)). A `RecorderObject` receiving 50 frames per second per speaker never hibernates during the meeting, and with the compatibility flag `durable_object_io_tasks_prevent_eviction` (default since 2026-10-01, the project's compatibility date) a pending Whisper call keeps the object in memory "up to 15 minutes per operation" ([Compatibility flags](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/configuration/compatibility-flags/)), all of it billed as 128 MB wall-clock. The STATUS open question "Do the Realtime SFU and Workers AI speech models work on the Workers Free plan?" therefore has a practical answer: Workers AI works, but a few hours of recorded audio per day stops transcription outright, and the recorder's duration alone eats a quarter of the daily DO budget per meeting-hour. Stay on Paid, as the cost model already assumes, and treat Free as a non-option rather than an open question. On Paid the recorder is cheap: incoming WebSocket messages bill at 20:1 and outgoing ones are free ([DO pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/)).

**The Pages front door costs twice and hides the client.** Pages Functions "are billed as Cloudflare Workers requests" ([Pages Functions pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/functions/pricing/)) and each call through a service binding counts as a subrequest ([Service bindings](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/bindings/service-bindings/)), so every `/api/*` and `/ws/*` request is billed once in `meet-felix-edge` and once in `meet-felix`. Workers Logs, gradual deployments and remote development are Workers-only ([Migrate from Pages](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/static-assets/migration-guides/migrate-from-pages/)), so the front door has no persistent logs. Cloudflare now says "Start new projects with Workers" ([Pages](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/)) while still listing "Custom domains outside Cloudflare zones" as unsupported on Workers, which is why the front door exists; the alternative is Cloudflare for SaaS with a Worker as origin, which needs an extra Cloudflare-hosted zone ([Worker as origin](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/cloudflare-for-saas/start/advanced-settings/worker-as-origin/)). Two things are unverified and matter: whether `CF-Connecting-IP` and `request.cf` survive the service binding (the header docs cover same-zone `fetch()` only, [HTTP request headers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/reference/http-request-headers)), which the per-IP join rate limit planned in D6 depends on, and whether WebSocket pass-through over a binding is supported at all; the project's own join test proves it works today, but no doc promises it. For the recorder this has a direct consequence: the SFU adapter's `wss://` endpoint should be the `workers.dev` hostname, not `meet.example.com`, so the media stream skips the undocumented hop and the double request billing (inference).

**KV's cache fights recycled words.** KV changes "may take up to 60 seconds or more to be visible in other global network locations", and "Negative lookups indicating that the key does not exist are also cached" ([How KV works](https://developers.cloudflare.com/kv/concepts/how-kv-works/)). A guest who opens a link seconds after the owner created it, from an edge that already cached the miss, gets "not found" for up to a minute; and because D6 releases words at the end of a meeting, a word freed and re-issued within that window can resolve to the old `RoomObject` at some edges. KV allows one write per key per second with a 429 beyond that ([KV write](https://developers.cloudflare.com/kv/api/write-key-value-pairs/)), so the meetings index in `crates/worker/src/meetings.rs`, a read-modify-write of one key that the module already documents as lossy, can also fail outright when a creation and a settle land in the same second. The cheap mitigations are to ask the `RoomObject` for a word when KV misses (a stub lookup is an internal subrequest, 1,000 per invocation on Free, [Changelog 2026-02-11](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-02-11-subrequests-limit/)) and to never reissue a word within a minute of its release. KV also supports no jurisdiction ([DLS compatibility](https://developers.cloudflare.com/data-localization/compatibility/)), and the index stores each meeting's `TranscriptRef`, so the token that unlocks a transcript lives outside the EU pin of D5; that is a note for the privacy notice, not a change, since the token alone is not personal data.

The remaining Durable Object facts mostly confirm the design. The compatibility date 2026-10-01 already covers the dangerous flags: `web_socket_auto_reply_to_close` (2026-04-07) removes the trap where clients saw close code 1006 unless the handler called `ws.close()` ([DO base class](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/base/)), and `delete_all_deletes_alarm` (2026-02-24) is what `RoomObject::alarm` relies on ([DO storage API](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/storage-api/)). Alarms are at-least-once with six exponential retries only on an uncaught exception, the constructor runs before the alarm handler after inactivity, and `getAlarm()` returns `null` while an alarm runs ([DO alarms](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/alarms/)); `blockConcurrencyWhile` has a 30-second timeout that resets the object ([DO State API](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/state/)). The `RoomObject` reloads its record for every queued event and stores before running effects, so the input-gate interleaving the docs warn about ([DO storage API](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/storage-api/)) is handled. One workers-rs fact deserves a decision: "Rust panics in Workers compile with `panic=abort`, which terminates the WebAssembly instance" unless built with `worker-build --panic-unwind`, which needs nightly prerequisites that v0.8.1+ installs automatically ([workers-rs README](https://github.com/cloudflare/workers-rs)); the build command in `wrangler.toml` has no `--panic-unwind`, so any panic in `RoomObject` resets the object and drops every socket. Observability is cheap now but changes: each log is capped at 256 KB ([Workers Logs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/observability/logs/workers-logs/)), `wrangler tail` holds WebSocket-handler logs "until the WebSocket client closes the connection" ([Real-time logs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/observability/logs/real-time-logs/)), DO ids are logged outside the jurisdiction ([DO data location](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/reference/data-location/)), and from 2026-12-01 Workers Logs move to Observability pricing with 0.5 GB per day on Free ([Observability pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/observability/pricing/)); with `head_sampling_rate = 1`, never log a `RoomState` or a token. Finally, Workers AI "always run[s] remotely" in `wrangler dev` and such calls "will incur standard operational costs" ([Development & testing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/development-testing/)) and count against the 720 requests per minute ASR limit ([Workers AI limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/limits/)), so spike loops should use a mock `Transcriber`.

## The recorder must survive holes, restarts and silence

D16 and D17 already know the adapter reconnects for 15 seconds and that partial success per entry is normal. The docs add what the reconnect does not promise: "Audio egress uses a short, bounded backlog, and older frames are dropped when it fills" and "Recovery does not guarantee gapless or exactly-once delivery" ([WebSocket adapter](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/media-transport-adapters/websocket-adapter/)). Because a deploy restarts every DO and drops every WebSocket ([DO WebSockets best practice](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/best-practices/websockets/)), every deploy during a recorded meeting puts a hole into every speaker's audio. The `RecorderObject` therefore needs three rules in `meeting-core` terms: detect a hole from a jump in `sequenceNumber` or `timestamp` and close the chunk there with a fresh wall-clock `t0` instead of concatenating, otherwise every later time in that chunk drifts earlier; drop a frame whose `sequenceNumber` is not greater than the last one seen, since duplicates after a reconnect are allowed; and compute times inside a chunk from the sample count after a single `Date.now()` at its first frame, not from repeated clock reads. The `timestamp` field is described only as "for synchronization" with no clock rate, and the frame size, the behaviour on source-track end and the behaviour during silence are all undocumented ([WebSocket adapter](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/sfu/media-transport-adapters/websocket-adapter/)); spike 2 has to measure them, and the chunker must buffer input and cut encoder frames of 960 input samples independently of inbound message boundaries. Two more adapter facts are confirmed: adapter requests "do not support custom WebSocket headers", the token in the path is the only authentication, and the same URL is reused on reconnect so "its credential must stay valid across retries" (same page), which means the full adapter URL must never reach the logs; and "Retrying the whole creation batch can create duplicate resources", so each successful `adapterId` must be stored before any failed entry is retried, or one speaker's audio arrives twice and egress doubles.

Inside the DO, the risks are the restart and the subrequest limits. The open chunk (up to 30 seconds of Opus packets) exists only in WASM memory; on eviction, crash or deploy it is gone, and D16's "hibernation can lose at most the current partial chunk" understates how often that happens during active development. The cheap insurance is to append the encoded packets of the open chunk to DO SQLite every second or two (rows up to 2 MB, writes billed per row, [DO limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/); [DO pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/)) and rebuild the Ogg chunk on restart. R2 writes count as subrequests and only six connections can be open at once per invocation ([Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)), so a `put` must never be awaited inside the frame handler; queue chunks and drain them in a separate task with the queue head persisted. R2 allows one write per second to the same key with 429 beyond ([R2 limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/platform/limits/)), which the one-key-per-chunk layout in D16 already respects as long as a retry never re-puts the same key within a second. The EU bucket requires `jurisdiction = "eu"` on the binding and jurisdiction endpoints cannot reach other resources ([R2 data location](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/reference/data-location/)); the `r2_buckets` binding is not in `wrangler.toml` yet, so this belongs in the phase-3 checklist. For the transcript job, the alarm handler has a 15-minute wall-clock limit and retries at most six times on an uncaught error ([DO alarms](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/api/alarms/)); a persistent 429 from Workers AI thrown out of `alarm()` stalls the whole transcript, so the loop must catch inside the handler, persist per-chunk status, process a bounded batch behind a stored cursor and reschedule itself. D17 says every step is idempotent; it must also say that a failure is recorded, not thrown. Memory is not the problem at 128 MB per isolate ([Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)), but the runtime documents no backpressure for inbound frames, so a leak or an unbounded queue is the failure mode to test (inference).

The encoding path has three correctness traps that no spike has hit yet. Decimating 48 to 16 kHz by taking every third sample aliases everything above 8 kHz into the speech band; the low-pass must run before decimation, computed polyphase so only every third output is evaluated, with FIR history kept per speaker across frames ([Wikipedia: Downsampling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downsampling_(signal_processing))). Ogg/Opus granule positions count 48 kHz samples "regardless of the decoder's output rate", so each 20 ms packet from a 16 kHz encoder adds 960, not 320; pre-skip is subtracted from granules, "a pre-skip of at least 3,840 samples (80 ms) is RECOMMENDED", and the end-of-stream page trims the tail ([RFC 7845](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7845.html)). A wrong unit makes every chunk play at the wrong speed or report a third of its length. The encoder's lookahead should be read from the codec rather than hard-coded, since it "can vary by implementation, version, or configuration" ([Opus encoder CTLs](https://opus-codec.org/docs/opus_api-1.5/group__opus__encoderctls.html)), and `opus-rs` 0.1.37 documents no DTX, signal-type or lookahead controls ([opus-rs on docs.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/opus-rs/latest)), so the recorder gates silence itself and measures the delay once with an impulse. Playback of Ogg/Opus arrived in Safari only with 18.4 ([WebKit: Safari 18.4](https://webkit.org/blog/16574/webkit-features-in-safari-18-4/)), which limits any "play this segment" button on the transcript page to iOS 18.4 and later.

Whisper itself is the largest quality risk. On 301,317 pure non-speech inputs, large-v3 hallucinated 40.3% of the time, rising to **62.3% at 30 seconds of silence**, with "thank you" and "thanks for watching" as the top outputs; beam size 1 hallucinated least (19.5% against 28.2% at beam 5), and a Silero VAD cut hallucinations to 0.2% ([arXiv 2501.11378](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.11378)). D16 already flags silent chunks; the recorder must go further and define "non-silent" as a gate that was open for at least some hundreds of milliseconds, cut chunks only inside silence so no word is split at an edge (Whisper has no overlap-merge in this design), send `vad_filter: true`, try `beam_size` 1 or 2, drop chunks whose `transcription_info.duration_after_vad` is near zero and post-filter the known phrases; the German equivalents of the phrase list are an extrapolation, not sourced. The Workers AI schema confirms `initial_prompt`, `beam_size`, `vad_filter`, `condition_on_previous_text` and the per-segment `no_speech_prob`, `avg_logprob` and `compression_ratio` ([whisper-large-v3-turbo.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/production/src/content/workers-ai-models/whisper-large-v3-turbo.json)); because `initial_prompt` is per request, the glossary of participant names and terms has to be sent with every chunk or names will be spelled differently per chunk. Whisper processes "a sliding 30-second window" and detects one language per window ([openai/whisper README](https://github.com/openai/whisper)); the 30-second chunk limit in D16 fits that exactly, and spike 3's mixed-language success is encouraging, but an unadapted model reached up to 70% error on code-switched German/English in one thesis ([VUT thesis](https://dspace.vut.cz/items/bebb98ee-428b-4f4e-88ca-75b43d44a2b1), secondary), so `transcription_info.language` and `language_probability` belong in `transcript.json` per chunk for a later re-run with `language` forced. The maximum input per request is undocumented; the 30-second chunk is far below Cloudflare's own 1 MB tutorial chunks ([Whisper chunking tutorial](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/guides/tutorials/build-a-workers-ai-whisper-with-chunking/)). Nova-3 costs ten times more ($0.0052 per minute over HTTP) and its `diarize` buys nothing with per-speaker tracks ([nova-3](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/models/nova-3/)); it stays the fallback behind the `Transcriber` trait. The merge is unsourced design reasoning: group each speaker's segments into turns, sort turns by `t0` plus offset, mark overlaps rather than interleaving words, key speakers by the `meeting-core` participant identity rather than the SFU `sessionId` so a rejoin does not create a second speaker, and number duplicate display names deterministically.

## German law wants a documented consent and a deletion date

The design's consent gate is stronger than most tools' and still short of what German guidance asks for in two places. § 201 StGB punishes recording "das nichtöffentlich gesprochene Wort eines anderen auf einen Tonträger" without authorisation ([§ 201 StGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__201.html)), and the GDD's January 2026 paper on transcription states that because tools buffer audio, "technisch regelmäßig eine Aufnahme vor[liegt]", that criminal law accepts implied consent while data-protection law needs "eine ausdrückliche, dokumentierte Einwilligung", and that the information must cover purpose, scope, retention, recipients and "die eingesetzten Systeme (z.B. KI-gestützte Transkriptionssoftware)" ([GDD Kurzpapier 4](https://www.gdd.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/GDD-Kurzpapier-4-Gespraechstranskription.pdf)). Heuking's 2026 note agrees that joining a labelled meeting can be consent "but only with clear and transparent information" ([Heuking Fachbeitrag](https://www.heuking.de/de/news-events/newsletter-fachbeitraege/artikel/rechtssicherer-einsatz-von-ki-transkriptionstools-in-unternehmen.html)). D15's "Join and accept recording" button is the explicit act, close to Nextcloud Talk's checkbox before joining ([Nextcloud Talk call recording](https://docs.nextcloud.com/server/stable/user_manual/en/talk/call_recording.html)); what is missing is the record and the notice. `RoomState` holds only `consent_recording: bool`; **it should hold when consent was given and which notice version was shown, and that record belongs in `transcript.json`.** The pre-join notice has to name the owner as controller, Cloudflare as processor, the retention period, the fact that everyone with the link can read the transcript (D19), and the transfer: Workers AI is "Not compatible" with Regional Services ([DLS compatibility](https://developers.cloudflare.com/data-localization/compatibility/)) and Cloudflare's subprocessor list names CoreWeave in the United States and Nebius in England for Workers AI ([Cloudflare sub-processors](https://www.cloudflare.com/gdpr/subprocessors/cloudflare-services/)). D5 already says audio may be processed outside the EU; the subprocessor list makes it concrete enough for an Art. 13 notice, which must list recipients and third-country transfers with their safeguards ([Art. 13 DSGVO](https://dsgvo-gesetz.de/art-13-dsgvo/)). The consent dialog mid-meeting is also covered: the DSK wants participants warned "entweder durch einen expliziten und durch die Teilnehmenden zu bestätigenden Hinweis oder durch Kennzeichnung innerhalb der Benutzerschnittstelle", recording off by default and only moderators able to start it ([DSK Orientierungshilfe Videokonferenzsysteme](https://www.Datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/20201023_oh_videokonferenzsysteme.pdf), 2020, the newest DSK guidance found); `ConsentRequired` plus the red badge from D28 meets that, though the default `RecordingPolicy::FromStart` is the opposite of the DSK's "deaktiviert in der Voreinstellung" and is worth a sentence in D15 explaining why a transcript tool differs.

Retention is the clearest conflict with a decision. D18 keeps audio and transcripts indefinitely; the GDD says audio "sollten … nach der Fertigstellung des Transkripts gelöscht werden" and that revoked consent requires immediate deletion ([GDD Kurzpapier 4](https://www.gdd.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/GDD-Kurzpapier-4-Gespraechstranskription.pdf)), and the DSK expects content deleted "regelmäßig unverzüglich nach dem Abschluss der Videokonferenz" absent another legal basis ([DSK Orientierungshilfe](https://www.Datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/20201023_oh_videokonferenzsysteme.pdf)). The minimal change to D18 is to delete the Ogg chunks once `transcript.json` exists (an R2 lifecycle rule can do this by prefix and age, [R2 lifecycles](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/buckets/object-lifecycles/)), to state a transcript retention in the notice, and to give the host a delete button, which the Art. 17 rights of every speaker in the transcript require anyway. The token link of D9 is defensible only with hygiene around it, because the guidance requires that "Nur berechtigte Personen" reach recordings: `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` and `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` on `/t/{token}`, the token in the path and never in a query string, no URL in logs, and expiry tied to retention. Those are inferences from the guidance, not sourced practice, and whether a German court accepts a pre-join tap as Art. 7(1) documentation has no case law either way. None of this is legal advice; for a one-owner private tool the owner is the controller personally once transcripts are shared by link.

## Conclusion

The pattern across all three areas is the same: the design handles the documented happy path and the documented failure codes well, and the gaps sit where Cloudflare or the browsers make no promise at all. The SFU does not promise gapless adapter delivery, the runtime does not promise that an old DO can read a new record, the browsers do not promise that audio plays after a silent reconnect, and Whisper does not promise silence in, silence out. The right response is not more retries but explicit state: a version on the stored record, a consent record with a timestamp, a chunk boundary at every hole, a per-chunk status in the transcript job. Each of these is a `meeting-core` type, which fits D20 and makes the shells simpler, not more defensive.

Two decisions deserve an explicit update rather than a quiet fix. D18 should say audio is deleted when the transcript exists and transcripts expire after a stated period, because the notice cannot be written without a number. D15 should say what the consent record contains and that the pre-join notice, not the in-meeting badge, is where the Art. 13 information lives. With those two and the one-line `priorityOrdering` fix, the remaining work is measurement: spike 2 for the adapter's clock and frame size, spike 4 for whether the 2020-era iOS bugs still bite on iOS 26, and a real meeting on the dashboard for egress.
